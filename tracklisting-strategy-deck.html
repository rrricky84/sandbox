Tracklisting strategy · July 2026

Hear it. Keep it.
Play it forward.

Why track metadata is Mixcloud's most under-leveraged asset —
and the six fronts we have to move together, not one at a time.

The paradox

We pitch Mixcloud at music obsessives.
We deliver a more passive listening experience than the mainstream platforms we define ourselves against.

A Spotify listener knows every track they hear, can save it, and it feeds their recommendations. A Mixcloud listener hears an hour of music that washes over them — usually without a single named track. The music is deeper; the metadata experience is shallower.

Current state

We run two parallel track-metadata systems.
Neither talks to the other — and discovery sees neither.

System 1 · Creator tracklists

What DJs type in

Manual entry of artist / track / timestamp per row, stored as Sections against globally deduped Artist & Track entities. Low uptake.

mixcloud/core/music/models.py — Section, Track, Artist
System 2 · Fingerprinting

What our vendors hear

ACRCloud (primary) + Gracenote (legacy) identify tracks in every upload — billions of samples — purely for rights enforcement and royalty reporting. Staff-only.

mixcloud/core/track_identification/ — CloudcastSample
Where neither lands · Discovery

What listeners can find

Elasticsearch indexes a show's title and owner name — nothing from either system reaches it. No track names, no artist names, no artist pages, no track saving.

mixcloud/core/search/indexers.py — serialize_cloudcast

The fingerprint-derived tracklist and the creator-entered tracklist are never reconciled — even our own licensing enforcement (SRPC) ignores what the DJ typed and trusts only the fingerprints.

System 1 · Creator tracklists

The supply side is manual, unmeasured,
and untouched for years

The discovery gap

Discovery is blind to the music inside the mixes

  • Full-text search matches only show title + owner name. Descriptions are explicitly excluded; track and artist names were never indexed. mixcloud/core/search/query.py — _build_cloudcasts_query
  • The plumbing for artist-led discovery already half-exists: artist IDs are indexed as discover tokens (a:<artist_id>), and the deprecated REST API still has working "Hot shows featuring X" endpoints — nothing in the product consumes them. search/indexers.py · api/artist/views.py — ArtistHot/New/Popular
  • Listeners can save shows (Listen Later, favorites) but not tracks — there is no track-level or artist-level action anywhere in the product. mixcloud/core/listen_later/
  • We detect when someone searches for a popular artist name… and show them a purpose message instead of results. graphql/models/search.py — show_purpose_message

The thesis

One canonical tracklist per show — assembled from every source we have, confidence-scored, and spent three ways: listener experience, creator value, and label reporting.

Tracklist coverage is our metadata supply chain. Every initiative below is a way to raise coverage or to spend it. Solving supply without experience wastes the data; solving experience without supply ships an empty feature. They only work together.

The core claim

Move any one of these alone and the listener feels nothing.

Effortless supply · Rising hit rate · Fingerprints in the product · Creator incentives · Licensing headroom · Listener payoff

A package, not a menu.

Pillar 1 · Supply

Make the tracklist automatic,
not an act of devotion

  • Ship the desktop app. The hackweek Serato/Rekordbox ingestion project is the single biggest coverage lever — take it to production and treat local DJ-software history as a first-class upload source.
  • Close the web-import gaps. Add Rekordbox alongside Serato/Traktor/Cue; accept sections in the upload mutation itself instead of the post-hoc second call. js/www/services/tracklistImport/ · graphql/mutations/upload_cloudcast.py
  • Assist the editor. Autocomplete against existing Artist/Track entities; pre-fill rows from fingerprint matches ("we heard these — confirm?") so the DJ edits instead of types.
  • Instrument adoption. Tracklist attach-rate and timestamp-rate become dashboard metrics — today we can't even quantify the problem. Only proxy today: Cloudcast.is_timestamped
Pillar 2 · Truth

Reconcile the two tracklists we already have —
and get a third source for free

A resolved-tracklist layer that merges creator Sections with fingerprint samples — and, as Trackdrop watermarking lands, deterministic watermark detections — row by row, with source and confidence on each entry.

Creator confirms machine

Fingerprint matches become editor suggestions; a creator's confirmation upgrades a ~60% probabilistic match into ground truth — and corrects the misses.

Machine enriches creator

Typed tracklists get timestamps, ISRCs and canonical artist links from the fingerprint layer — turning free text into linkable entities.

Reporting gets cleaner

Every confirmed row shrinks the unidentified share that manual studies exist to estimate — reducing a real, documented cost and pain point.

docs/schema/manual_study.md · utils.py merge_tracks TODO: "Reporting team hates…"

One source of truth

SRPC enforcement, the listener tracklist and royalty reports currently read three different answers to "what's in this mix". They should read one.

restrictions/utils.py — recompute_srpc_restriction_info

Watermarks and fingerprints cover each other's blind spots: fingerprinting fails hardest on unreleased promo material — exactly the tracks Trackdrop watermarks. Detection is a yes/no read, not a probabilistic match.

Pillar 3 · Experience

Let listeners keep what they hear

  • Reveal as heard. Tracklist rows unlock as playback passes them — the licensing-safe version of "what's this track?", for everyone, not just subscribers. Builds on the existing now-playing machinery. js/shared/relay/utils/getCurrentTrack.js
  • My track history. A personal, private log of every identified track you've heard — the artefact that turns a session into a crate-digging trip.
  • Save the track, follow the artist. Track-level saves feed artist-led discovery: "more shows featuring this artist" — the indexed artist tokens and legacy endpoints prove the query already works. search discover_tag a:<artist_id> · api/artist/views.py
  • Creators get credited. Artist pages aggregate the shows that feature them — a reason for artists and labels to want tracklists on Mixcloud.

Upfront tracklists remain the subscriber perk — reveal-as-heard doesn't cannibalise it; it advertises it.

North star

Where this goes: lineage

Everywhere else, music arrives with no story — a song with no past. Mixcloud can sell the opposite: where a track came from, who made it, who played it, who put you onto it, and who you pass it to next.

Today · Permanence

The archive. Sets live forever, legally — the foundation everything else stands on.

Next · Depth

This strategy. Listeners leave every session with the music they heard — revealed, kept, saved. The missing middle between today and the vision.

Then · Lineage

Every saved track remembers who put you onto it and through which show. Listeners graduate into selectors. Taste has a bloodline, and you have a place in it.

And lineage runs upstream too. Trackdrop watermarks every promo it delivers — so when that record surfaces in a Mixcloud mix, we can tell the label which DJ played it and which fans heard it. Attribution becomes a product we sell to rights-holders, not just a report we owe them — and it funds the label trust this whole strategy spends.

Trackdrop ↔ Mixcloud analytics handoff already live: mixcloud/core/analytics/events.py
Risks & the ask

What could bite, and what I'm asking for

Risks to hold

· Label deals must explicitly cover post-listen reveal & personal history — verify before building.
· Fingerprints can expose unreleased edits/dubs a DJ meant to keep secret — creators need a per-show veto.
· Match quality below ~60% makes a bad suggestion UX — confidence thresholds matter more than coverage.

The ask

· Sanction a discovery sprint on the resolved-tracklist layer.
· Commit the desktop app to a production timeline.
· Instrument adoption metrics this quarter.
· Open the label conversation on the two green-lit-but-unbuilt surfaces.

The data, the pipelines and half the plumbing already exist.
What's missing is the decision to point them at the listener.
